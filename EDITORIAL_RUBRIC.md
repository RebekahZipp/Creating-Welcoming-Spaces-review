# Editorial Rubric

This repository separates proposal-level scoring from collection-level editorial judgment.

## Proposal-level criteria

Scores use a four-point scale with no neutral midpoint:

- **1 — Does not yet meet**
- **2 — Partially meets**
- **3 — Meets well**
- **4 — Exceeds**

### Contribution — 35%

Does the proposal make a meaningful contribution to the scholarly or professional conversation? What will a reader understand, see differently, or think more deeply about?

### Adaptability — 25%

Can librarians in other contexts draw useful lessons, methods, frameworks, or practices from the chapter?

### Grounding — 20%

Is the proposal grounded in an appropriate and credible basis, such as research, theory, assessment, reflective practice, lived experience, or professional expertise?

### Clarity — 10%

Is the proposed chapter's focus, key ideas, and direction clear enough to understand the chapter being proposed?

### Lasting Relevance — 10%

Will the chapter remain useful beyond the immediate institution, project, technology, or moment?

## Collection-level questions

These questions are not converted automatically into a numerical score.

1. **Strengthen** — Does the proposal make the collection stronger as an integrated work?
2. **Duplicate** — Does another proposal cover substantially the same ground better?
3. **Gap** — Does the proposal fill an important thematic or methodological gap?
4. **Represent** — Does it broaden the volume's balance of institutions, populations, methods, perspectives, resource levels, or approaches?
5. **Return** — Is this a chapter readers are likely to cite, revisit, or use over time?

## Editorial principle

> A strong edited volume is a curated scholarly conversation, not a stack of good chapters.

Theme frequency, keyword counts, and thematic overlap are descriptive evidence only. They must not be converted automatically into rubric scores or editorial decisions.
